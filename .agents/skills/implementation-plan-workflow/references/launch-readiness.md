# Environment and commercial launch planning

The implementation plan covers the work needed to reach the agreed release endpoint, including operational setup, required documents, and market/commercial launch when applicable. A completed feature set or a successful deployment alone does not establish launch readiness. For a commercial product, include launch work; if the requested endpoint is a prototype or pilot, identify later launch dependencies and their status without executing or silently expanding that assignment.

## Assess launch coverage

Assess the categories below against the product, users, data, jurisdictions, selected stack, and launch model. Turn applicable work into dependency-ordered slices with outputs and completion evidence. Record a concise reason for a category that is not applicable, or an owner-accepted deferral and its effect on launch. Resolve consequential missing decisions with the owner; later unknowns can remain explicit blocked work. Choose tools from accepted requirements and operating constraints rather than adopting the examples automatically.

| Category | Work the plan must capture when applicable | Completion evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Environments and delivery | Provision local/test, staging, and production resources using the chosen hosting platforms, for example Railway or Vercel. Configure databases, storage, workers/jobs, environment variables and secrets, public versus server configuration, domains/TLS, auth redirects, webhooks, provider test/live modes, CI/CD and deployment access. Separate credentials/data by environment; define promotion, migrations, smoke checks, and rollback. | Each required environment deploys and passes its actual checks; isolation and provider callbacks are exercised; deployment/rollback instructions and configuration ownership are recorded without secret values. |
| Backup and restore | Define data/file coverage, recovery objectives, schedule, retention, protected storage, access ownership, failure alerts, and restore procedure. Plan provider configuration and an isolated restore exercise, including application/data checks and recovery from migration failure where relevant. | A backup succeeds and its data is restored and checked in an isolated target; observed recovery results, limits, and runbook are recorded. Enabling a backup setting is not restore evidence. |
| Error tracking and diagnosis | Configure the selected error tracker, for example Sentry, with environment/release tagging, source maps when needed, useful request/job correlation, sensitive-data redaction, actionable alerts, and incident ownership. | A controlled error from the relevant deployed boundary reaches the tracker and its intended alert recipient; diagnostic context is usable and sensitive data is excluded. |
| Uptime and service monitoring | Configure external checks, for example UptimeRobot, and relevant health/readiness checks, job/provider failure detection, alert destinations, thresholds, and response/escalation instructions. | A controlled test of failure/recovery proves the monitor and alert path; a running process is not evidence that required dependencies are healthy. |
| Product analytics | Choose meaningful launch outcomes, events, properties, identity/account handling, funnels, and dashboards using the selected analytics tool, for example PostHog. Define applicable consent, collection limits, retention, and environment separation; decide whether recording features are needed and how sensitive fields are protected. | Representative journeys produce the intended events and dashboard results; duplicates, excluded environments, and consent/privacy behavior are checked. |
| Privacy, data policy, and compliance documents | Plan drafting, review, and publication of applicable privacy/data policies, retention/deletion/export procedures, consent/cookie disclosures, terms, and sector/customer-required compliance or security documents. Capture actual data uses, subprocessors, responsibilities, access requests, and incident processes. Determine required documents and review requirements from the product's jurisdictions and obligations. | Drafts agree with actual product behavior and providers; designated reviews/approval and required publication or user-facing access are completed. Verified basis supports legal claims; a draft or checklist alone does not establish compliance. |
| Commercial and customer readiness | Include applicable pricing/entitlements, live billing and transaction checks, tax/invoicing/refund/cancellation requirements, provider/account approvals, customer onboarding, support/contact channels, user/admin help, and launch communications or storefront/listing assets. Identify domain, email/deliverability, budget/capacity limits, and dependencies on business-owner work. | Critical paid/customer journeys and support routes are demonstrated; required documents/assets and business-owner decisions are ready for the agreed launch channel. |
| Release and launch operations | Plan applicable security/access, accessibility, performance/load, migration and real-integration checks; pilot feedback resolution; launch go/no-go, deployment, post-deploy smoke tests, staged exposure, monitoring watch, rollback triggers, and support handoff. | Required checks and reviews have evidence, an owner makes the launch decision, production smoke/monitoring checks pass, and rollback/support procedures are usable. |

Sentry is an error-tracking example, UptimeRobot is an uptime-monitoring example, and PostHog is a product-analytics example. These capabilities can use different suppliers or existing infrastructure.

## Make non-feature work executable

Use the same slice contract as feature work. For external setup and launch work, also specify the target environment, required account/access, configuration names and purpose, dependencies, expected document/configuration output, and verification/review evidence. Record paid resources or external approvals as prerequisites and stay within user authorization; a plan does not authorize purchases, provisioning, legal approval, or publication.

Schedule enabling work when its dependents need it: staging before deployed integration/pilot checks; production/provider setup before live release checks; recovery and alert tests before the relevant launch gates; policy review after data/provider decisions and before required publication. Document preparation is work with a concrete output and completion criterion, not a vague instruction to be compliant.

Project preparation seeds applicable environment/setup, recovery, observability, analytics, policy, and launch context from the PRD and plan. Later slices finish configuration, verify real boundaries, draft/review required public documents, and carry out authorized launch work. Reference the authoritative document rather than keeping a second launch task list.

## Close the launch endpoint honestly

Keep configuration, draft, review, verification, publication, and operational readiness distinguishable in evidence. Record provider/access blockers, missing legal/business decisions, untested recovery, undelivered alerts, and failed checks against affected slices. Do not close the commercial-launch endpoint while required work remains; accepted deferrals must state what launch scope they permit. After launch, record observed deployment/monitoring results, support ownership, and the next agreed follow-up without claiming future checks already passed.
